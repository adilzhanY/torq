Three Stats redesigns

You're right that the page measures the wrong thing. Volume is how much work you did; rank and 1RM are how strong you got, and only the second one is why torq exists. All three ideas below delete volume from the headline. They differ in what replaces it: the ladder, the lifts, or the month.

What's wrong

1

Volume is the hero. "29k VOLUME (KG)" sits in the KPI row and the largest chart on the page is weekly volume. Volume rewards long sessions, not strong ones, you can add 20% volume by adding a set of curls.

2

Two charts, one story. Weekly volume and weekly workout count have the same shape from the same weeks, stacked. The second one is the first one, dimmer.

3

Four of six bars are empty. The month window always shows 5–6 weeks whether or not they contain anything, so the chart is mostly background.

4

The muscle breakdown renders as an empty grey strip. The legend says Legs 50% / Chest 19% / Back 15%, but the stacked bar itself has no visible colour. That's a real bug, not a design opinion.

5

Raw axis ticks: 35022, 23348, 11674. Unrounded numbers nobody can scan, on an axis whose job is orientation.

6

Nothing about getting stronger. No rank over time, no PR, no 1RM, no "which lift is stalling", and those are the only questions this app is uniquely able to answer.

Idea 1

RecommendedMedium build

The climb

The page becomes one question: am I getting stronger, and how far to the next tier? A rank-points line with the tier ladder painted behind it, then the two lifts closest to promotion in kilograms to go, then your recent records. Volume is demoted to a "Training load" row you can open if you actually want it.

What you gain

  • The chart the app has never drawn. Rank over time with tier bands turns nine abstract thresholds into a picture of a climb, and every point on it is already computable from your logs.
  • "+3.9 kg to Gold" is actionable. Points are the currency but kilos are what you do on Monday, so the tier-up rows are denominated in kilos.
  • Records get a home. PRs are currently visible only inside a finished-workout summary that scrolls away.
  • Body weight stays, not vanity, it's a DOTS input, so it belongs next to the rank it moves.

What it costs

  • Rank history has to be replayed. Points-at-a-date means recomputing rankLifts over every prefix of your history, cheap for a few hundred workouts, but it needs a memo, not a naive loop per pixel.
  • A new "Training load" sub-page for the volume/sets/hours that leave the surface.
  • The line looks flat for a brand-new user with two sessions.

Idea 2

Most specificLarge build

The lift lab

The page is about exercises, not sessions. It opens with a dumbbell chart, every ranked lift, 90 days ago versus today, which is the one form that makes "which lifts are moving and which are stuck" readable at a glance. Pick a lift and get its whole file: e1RM line with records, tier, percentile, rep maxes.

What you gain

  • Stalls become visible. A flat dumbbell row is the single most useful thing a training app can tell you, and nothing in torq currently says it.
  • Exactly what you asked for, "weight increasing in exercises", as the page's whole subject.
  • The rep-max table earns its place here, where you're already thinking about one lift.

What it costs

  • It duplicates a screen you already have. ExerciseInfo already ships About / History / Records / Charts / Rank per exercise. Building the same charts on Stats means two places to maintain the same thing, the strongest argument against this one.
  • Needs a selection model and a sensible default lift.
  • A lift needs two data points 90 days apart before the headline chart says anything.

Idea 3

DensestLarge build

The monthly scorecard

Keeps your month stepper, but every module changes subject: points gained instead of volume moved, the whole nine-tier ladder with your marker on it, what moved this month, and a strength-balance chart that names your lagging lift. Reads like a report, not a dashboard.

What you gain

  • The ladder finally appears. Nowhere in torq can you currently see all nine tiers and where you sit among them, the game's whole board.
  • Strength balance is a genuine coaching insight and no competitor of yours shows it: a 120 kg deadlift behind a 114 kg squat is a real finding.
  • The heatmap replaces two bar charts with one denser, better-filled picture of consistency.
  • The month stepper you built stops being wasted on volume.

What it costs

  • Most to build, five new chart forms, one of which (the balance baseline) is a convention you'd have to defend.
  • The balance chart is noise for many users. Anyone not training all three competition lifts gets a chart about lifts they don't do.
  • A month is a short window for strength: some months move 2 pts and the hero reads as failure.

The charts

Which form, and why not the obvious one

You asked what charts to use. The form follows the reader's job, not the data's shape, and in several places the honest answer is "not a chart".

The questionFormWhy not the obvious alternative
Am I climbing?Line + tier bands, one seriesNot multi-line. The other lifts are context; eight hues would bury the one line that matters. One series needs no legend, the title names it.
How far to the next tier?MeterNot a two-slice donut. A single ratio against a limit is a bar, and the number does most of the work.
Which lifts moved?Dumbbell (before → after)Not grouped bars: pairs read as pairs and the gap is the subject. One hue in two treatments, hollow then, filled now.
How did one lift progress?Line, PR points ringedNot bars, time is continuous. The ring is secondary encoding so records aren't colour-alone.
Am I balanced?Diverging bars around a neutral centreNot a radar. Radar's filled area exaggerates magnitude and its axis order is arbitrary. Δ-to-target is textbook diverging.
Am I consistent?Calendar heatmap, one hueNot a weekly bar chart with four empty slots. A heatmap is dense where bars are sparse.
What records did I set?A listNot a chart at all. Events with names and dates are a feed; plotting them adds nothing.
Where is my body weight going?Small lineKeep it, DOTS divides by bodyweight, so it is the one measurement that moves your rank.
Which muscles did I train?Cut it, or horizontal stacked barToday it renders as an invisible grey strip. Either fix the fills or admit it was volume-by-another-name.

One rule I'd hold across all three: never put kilograms and points on one plot. A second y-axis makes the reader believe in a correlation whose alignment you invented. Two measures → two charts, or index both to 100 at the start.

My read: build 1, and steal one chart from 2. The rank line is the picture torq has been missing since the rank engine shipped, it's the only screen that would make someone open the app on a rest day. But idea 2's dumbbell chart is the cheapest big win on this page: two numbers per lift, and it answers "what's stalling", which idea 1 only implies. The rest of idea 2 I'd leave alone, because ExerciseInfo already owns per-lift detail and a second copy is a second thing to keep true. Idea 3's ladder is worth stealing later; its balance chart wants a real opinion about ratios before it ships.
