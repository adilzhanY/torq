What goes wrong

1

Four screens in a trench coat. Identity, rank, settings and account deletion share one scroll with nothing but hairlines between them.

2

Settings are buried behind the trophy. Switching kg → lb means scrolling past a 168 px shield you did not come for.

3

The rank block is now a duplicate. Ranks is a dock tab with a bigger version of the same shield, the same points and the same best-lift list.

4

It is not a profile. Your @handle, your friends and your Arena standing (the things that make it yours) are all on another tab.

5

Developer tools ship to users. "Seed demo workouts" sits three taps from a real person's real history.

6

Nothing here is shareable, even though ShareRankCard already exists and this is the page people screenshot.

Idea 1

RecommendedMedium build

The athlete card

Profile stops being a settings screen and becomes the page you would screenshot: avatar, name, handle, a rank strip that summarises rather than repeats, three lifetime numbers, your best lifts with their percentiles, and a Share button wired to the card component that already exists. Everything adjustable moves behind a gear.

What you gain

  • It finally answers "who am I here". Handle, tier, streak and best lifts in one glance, above the fold.
  • Settings get their own page and stop competing with a trophy for attention.
  • The rank block stops duplicating the Ranks tab. It becomes a 64 px summary that links there.
  • Share reuses ShareRankCard, so the most postable screen in the app finally has a share button.
  • Developer tools move to the settings page, out of a normal user's path.

What it costs

  • A new Settings screen to build and route to, the ~8 sections that move have to keep working.
  • Changing units is now two taps instead of one long scroll. Better, but it is a change.
  • "Lifting since" and the streak need wiring in; the streak already exists, the join date does not.

Idea 2

Most disciplinedSmall–medium build

Settings hub

The opposite bet: accept that this is the settings screen and make it a great one. A compact identity row on top, then grouped rows that each show their current value and open a focused sub-page. The rank leaves entirely. It already has a whole tab.

What you gain

  • Everything is visible without opening anything. Each row previews its value, so "what unit am I on" is answered by scanning, not tapping.
  • It scales. New settings are new rows, not new 200-line blocks in one file.
  • No duplication left, one shield in the app, on the Ranks tab.
  • Toggles that belong inline (sound, notifications) stay inline; everything heavier gets a page.
  • Cheapest of the three: mostly moving existing blocks into sub-pages.

What it costs

  • No identity, no pride. This page will never be screenshotted, and a rank app arguably wants one screen that is about you.
  • Several sub-pages to build, each needing its own back handling.
  • Notifications toggle implies a settings switch that does not exist yet. It is currently permission-only.

Idea 3

Most motivatingLarge build

Trophy room

Profile as a public showcase: tier-tinted hero, shield overlapping the avatar, a shelf of earned and still-locked trophies, percentile lines on your best lifts, your friends' faces, and a "Preview as friend" switch that shows exactly what other people see. The strongest reason to open the app on a rest day.

What you gain

  • A retention surface. Locked trophies with progress ("128 / 200") are the only thing here that pulls someone back mid-week.
  • "Preview as friend" makes the privacy model visible, the opt-in design finally has a place where you can check it, not just trust it.
  • The most shareable screen you could build, and it feeds the existing share card directly.
  • Percentiles get a home on the profile, where the claim is most persuasive.

What it costs

  • Achievements do not exist. They need defining, storing, unlock detection, sync, and a notification, a feature in their own right, not a redesign.
  • Badge inflation is a real risk. Cheap trophies would undercut the tier system, which is the thing the whole app is built on.
  • Two profile states (yours / as-a-friend) doubles what has to be tested.
  • Settings still need somewhere to go, so this is Idea 1's settings page plus the shelf.

My read: 1 and 2 are not really rivals, 1 contains 2, because the athlete card needs a settings page and that page should be built the way idea 2 describes. So the honest question is whether Profile should be about you at all, or stay a drawer of knobs. In a rank app with friends and an Arena, it should be about you: build 1, and make its Settings page look like 2. 3 is the right destination but the wrong next step, trophies are a feature to design on their own terms, and shipping shallow ones would cheapen the tier ladder the whole product rests on.
