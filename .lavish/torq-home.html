Volume is gone from all three — you're right that nobody measures it. These three differ in what Home is organised around — the day, the week, or your numbers.

What's wrong

1

A rest day looks like a training day. Both render as an eyebrow, a headline and a grey sentence. The only difference is the word itself and a 17 px moon — you have to read to know what today is, and Home's whole job is that you shouldn't have to.

2

Volume owns the only chart. "VOLUME · LAST 7 DAYS · 29185" with a full-width line, measuring the thing you don't care about.

3

The date scrubber earns little. It costs ~90 px to say "it is the 9th" — which the header already says above it — and the tick marks carry no information about which days you train.

4

Almost no stats. One rank block. Your streak is a pill, PRs are on Stats, tier-ups are a single grey sentence.

5

Nothing says what today would have trained. On a rest day you're told "Next up: Monday — Push Day" but never which muscles, how long, or what you did last time.

Idea 1

RecommendedMedium build

Today, full-bleed

The day becomes a panel, not a sentence. A training day is lime-framed with the session name, the muscles it hits, its length and one big Start. A rest day is a different object entirely — grey, moonlit, no CTA — and it uses the space to tell you what is recovering and what lands tomorrow. The date scrubber goes; the week strip does that job better by showing what each day trains.

What you gain

  • You know the day from across the room. Colour, frame, icon and the presence or absence of a CTA all change — not just the noun.
  • A rest day stops being an empty screen. What is recovering, how long since you trained it, what is next: the day earns its space instead of apologising for itself.
  • The week strip says PUSH / PULL / LEGS, so "what day is today" and "what is coming" are one glance.
  • Three stat tiles replace the volume chart with things that move: rank, streak, kilos to the next tier.

What it costs

  • The date scrubber goes. Scrubbing to an arbitrary past day moves to the calendar button — a tap instead of a swipe.
  • Muscle chips need a body-part rollup per routine, and "recovering since" needs days-since-last-trained per group. Both are computable, neither exists yet.
  • A big lime panel is a lot of accent on a page that is otherwise cardless.

Idea 2

Most planner-ishMedium build

The week board

Home is the week, not the day. Seven cards across the top replace the date scrubber entirely; today is simply the selected one, and tapping any other day shows what you did or will do then. Under it, the week's numbers — sessions against plan, time, rank gained, records.

What you gain

  • The scrubber becomes useful. Same gesture cost, but each cell now carries a state instead of a tick mark.
  • Weekly stats are the honest window for a 3–4 session-a-week lifter — daily numbers are mostly zeroes.
  • Answers "am I on plan this week" instantly, which is the question a plan-based app should lead with.

What it costs

  • Today is smaller. It's one selected cell rather than the page's subject — the opposite of what you asked for if the day is what matters most.
  • Seven tappable cells at 46 dp are under the 48 dp touch minimum on a 360 dp screen.
  • Needs per-day session lookup for past AND future days, plus an empty state for days with neither.

Idea 3

Most statsSmall build

The stat wall

The opposite bet: today shrinks to a one-line banner and the page becomes six stat tiles — rank, streak, week, next tier, last record, body weight. Cheapest of the three, because every number already exists somewhere in the app; this only gathers them.

What you gain

  • Most stats per screen, which is half of what you asked for, and every tile is already computable today.
  • Smallest build. No muscle rollups, no recovery model — just layout over data that exists.
  • The banner still colour-codes rest vs training, so the day is clearer than it is now even though it is smaller.

What it costs

  • The day loses. You asked for MORE visual weight on today's session and this gives it less — a 62 px banner.
  • Six tiles is a lot of boxes for a design system whose rule is "content on the page, not in cards".
  • Nothing here is a call to action; Home stops prompting you to train.

My read: build 1, and take idea 3's tile row with it — that is what the two halves of your ask actually add up to. 1 is the only one that makes a rest day and a training day look like different things rather than different words, and it is the only one that uses a rest day for anything. 3's tiles are nearly free, so three of them belong under 1's hero; all six would turn a cardless page into a box grid. 2 is the one I would not build: it makes today smaller, and its seven tappable cells land at 46 dp, under the touch-target floor we just spent the nav redesign fixing.
